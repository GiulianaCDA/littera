Perform a complete linguistic "autopsy" of the current Roman Letter.

Divide the analysis into:

### 1. Letter overview
Explain: who wrote it; who received it; the main subjects; the emotional/social/political tone; what kind of English the translation uses.

### 2. English worth stealing
Select useful expressions and sentence patterns from the letter. For each one: original expression; natural Portuguese meaning; why it is useful; whether it sounds natural today; one modern example. Prioritize expressions realistically reusable.

### 3. Vocabulary for Anki
Select the most useful vocabulary — do NOT add every difficult word. Classify each word as:
- ACTIVE — worth using;
- RECOGNITION — useful mainly for reading;
- LITERARY/ARCHAIC — interesting but not necessary for active use.

For each: English; Portuguese meaning; part of speech; short example; whether common, formal, literary, or rare. At the end, provide an Anki-ready section.

### 4. Hidden grammar
Find the important grammar structures. For each: quote the relevant sentence; identify the grammar; explain the structure simply; explain WHY that tense/structure was used; compare with a simpler alternative when useful; give 1–3 modern examples.

Pay special attention to: have/has + past participle; had + past participle; have/has been; would; passive voice; modal verbs; infinitive vs gerund; prepositions; relative clauses; conditionals; conjunctions.

### 5. How would I say this today?
Choose formal/literary sentences and rewrite them in (A) modern neutral English and (B) casual/conversational English when appropriate. Explain the difference.

### 6. Sentences to incorporate
Choose 5–10 sentence patterns to actively incorporate. For each: original; modern version if necessary; Portuguese meaning; one personalized example; a note about when it can be used.

### 7. Roman Letter Scoreboard
Update the cumulative scoreboard in `ROMAN_LETTERS_ENGLISH_TRACKER.md`: useful vocabulary learned; expressions learned; grammar structures learned; recurring structures; words/sentences added to Anki; words that have appeared again; structures to review. Do not inflate numbers by counting trivial variations of the same structure.
